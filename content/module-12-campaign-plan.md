## Objectives

- Convert this program into a dated plan with measurable milestones
- Set the small number of metrics that actually predict progress
- Pre-decide your responses to the things that go wrong
- Build the support structure that keeps you executing in month 20

## Write it as an OPORD

You already know how to plan an operation. Use the format you know.

### Situation

Where you are today. Honest numbers: credit score, gross monthly income, monthly debts, cash reserves, current housing cost, entitlement available, location, and expected moves in the next three years.

### Mission

One sentence, specific and dated. Not "build wealth through real estate."

> **Example:** "By 31 December 2029, own three VA-financed small multifamily properties totaling eight doors, producing $1,800/month in net cash flow, while maintaining six months of PITI reserves."

### Sustainment

Reserves, insurance, the trades list, the CPA, the attorney, the lender relationships.

### Command and signal

Who holds you accountable, and how often you report. A spouse, a battle buddy doing the same thing, a local investor meetup, a veteran real estate group. **Monthly, out loud, to another person.** This is the single highest-leverage line in the plan, and the one most people skip.

## The five metrics

Track these monthly. Nothing else.

1. **Offers written this month** — the leading indicator of everything
2. **Reserve account balance** — months of PITI covered
3. **Credit score and DTI** — your qualification capacity
4. **Doors owned / doors leased** — the actual scoreboard
5. **Net cash flow, trailing 3 months** — reality, not projection

:::warning The honest failure modes
Most people who set out to do this do not finish. The reasons are consistent and none of them is market conditions:

- **Never writing offers.** Endless analysis, no action.
- **Buying a bad deal out of impatience** in month eight.
- **Skipping reserves**, then a $9,000 furnace ends the plan.
- **Wrecking their credit or DTI** with a vehicle purchase mid-plan.
- **Under-screening a tenant** to fill a vacancy fast.
- **Quitting after property #1** because being a landlord was harder than the podcast implied.

Every one of these is a decision, not an accident. Which means every one is preventable.
:::

## Where to get help

- **VA Regional Loan Center: 1-877-827-3702** — free, authoritative, staffed by people who do this all day
- **[va.gov home loans](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/)** — the actual rules
- **VA loan technicians** — if you ever struggle with payments, call *before* you miss one; they can intervene with servicers
- **Local landlord associations** — leases, screening tools, legal updates, education
- **Veteran real estate investor groups and local meetups** — the accountability structure that keeps you executing
- **A CPA and a real estate attorney** in your state, engaged before you need them

:::mission Final word
You earned a financial instrument that most Americans cannot buy at any price: leverage without a down payment, without mortgage insurance, on up to four units, renewable across a career. Used once, it is a nice benefit. Used with a plan, executed honestly, over years, it is a portfolio that outlives your service.

The rules are public. The math is on this site. The only variable left is whether you write the offers.
:::
