## Your first job is tenant selection

Nothing you do afterward matters as much. One bad tenant can cost a year of cash flow, and when you live in the same building, it costs your peace as well.

A defensible screening process:

1. **Write objective criteria and publish them** — income at 2.5–3× rent, minimum credit score, no evictions in X years, verifiable rental history, no relevant criminal history consistent with local law
2. **Apply them identically to every applicant.** This is both the fair thing and your legal protection.
3. **Verify income** with pay stubs, an LES, offer letters, or bank statements — not screenshots
4. **Call the previous landlord**, not just the current one. The current landlord may want them gone.
5. **Run credit, eviction, and background checks** through a proper tenant screening service with applicant consent
6. **Meet them at a showing.** How someone treats the process predicts how they treat the property.
7. **Document every decision** with the criteria you applied

:::warning Fair housing is not optional
The Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex (including sexual orientation and gender identity per current HUD guidance), familial status, and disability. Many states and cities add protected classes — source of income (including housing vouchers and BAH), age, marital status, military or veteran status, and criminal history limits. Violations carry serious civil penalties.

Practical protections: written criteria applied uniformly, no "I'd prefer…" conversations, advertising that describes the property rather than the ideal tenant, and a documented reason for every denial. A short fair housing course is one of the highest-return hours you will spend.
:::

## The lease

Use a state-specific lease — a local landlord association or real estate attorney version, not a generic internet PDF. Confirm it addresses:

- Rent amount, due date, grace period, and late fee within statutory limits
- Security deposit amount, where it is held, and the statutory return timeline
- Utilities: exactly who pays what, and who holds each account
- Maintenance responsibilities and the request process
- Entry notice requirements (typically 24–48 hours, per state law)
- Pets, smoking, parking, guests, quiet hours
- Required disclosures: lead paint for pre-1978, mold, bedbug history, local rent regulations
- Subletting and short-term rental prohibition
- Renewal and termination terms

## Systems from day one

Build these while you have one property; they will not build themselves when you have four.

- **Separate bank account per property** — or at minimum a dedicated rental account. Never commingle with personal funds.
- **Online rent collection.** Automated, dated, documented. Cash creates disputes.
- **Accounting from month one.** Track income and expenses by property and category. You will need Schedule E figures for taxes and for the next lender.
- **A maintenance ledger** and a short list of vetted trades: plumber, electrician, HVAC, handyman, roofer. Find them before the emergency.
- **A CapEx reserve account** funded monthly from rent, untouched for anything else.
- **A written turnover checklist** — clean, paint, re-key, inspect, photograph, list.
- **Photograph everything at move-in and move-out**, timestamped, with the tenant signing the condition report.

:::tip The Schedule E connection
Two years of rental income reported on Schedule E is what allows a future lender to count that income when you qualify for property #3. Sloppy books do not just cost you at tax time — they cap your portfolio. Treat clean accounting as a financing strategy.
:::

## Living next to your tenants

The live-in landlord dynamic is real and manageable.

- **Set the relationship at signing.** Friendly, professional, responsive — not friends. You will eventually need to enforce a lease.
- **Channel all requests to one place** — a text line, an email, or a portal. Not conversations in the driveway at 9pm.
- **Respond fast to real problems.** Habitability issues are legal obligations and the fastest way to lose a good tenant is ignoring a leak.
- **Do not accept partial rent casually.** Have a written policy and apply it.
- **Keep your own unit's boundaries.** Quiet hours apply to you too; you set the building's culture.
- **Consider a management company at 25–30% occupancy of your time.** Budgeting 8–10% from the start means hiring one later costs you nothing new.

## Forcing appreciation

Your equity growth does not have to wait for the market. Value in small multifamily is driven substantially by rent, which you can influence.

| Action | Typical cost | Typical effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Submeter or bill back utilities (RUBS, where legal) | $0–$4,000 | +$40–$90/unit/month to NOI |
| In-unit laundry or shared coin/card laundry | $1,500–$6,000 | +$25–$75/unit/month |
| Paint, flooring, lighting, hardware on turnover | $3,000–$8,000/unit | +$75–$200/unit/month |
| Kitchen and bath refresh | $8,000–$20,000/unit | +$150–$350/unit/month |
| Adding a legal bedroom where layout allows | $5,000–$15,000 | +$150–$300/month |
| Storage, parking, or pet rent | $0–$2,000 | +$25–$75/unit/month |
| Converting unfinished basement to a legal unit | $40,000+ | A whole new door — permits required |

At a 6% cap rate, **every $100/month of added NOI is roughly $20,000 of value.** That is the arithmetic that makes renovation work worth doing deliberately rather than randomly.

## VA renovation and energy financing

Two tools most veterans never hear about:

### VA Renovation / Alteration and Repair Loan

A purchase loan that includes funds for repairs and improvements, based on the property's **as-completed** value. Not every VA lender offers it, and it requires VA-approved contractors and a defined scope with inspections and draws. It is the tool for a property that is structurally sound but needs work — and it lets you buy properties other VA buyers must pass on, which means less competition.

Limitations to expect: it is not a ground-up rehab product, cosmetic-to-moderate work is the sweet spot, and timelines run longer than a standard purchase. Ask your lender candidly whether they have actually closed one.

### Energy Efficient Mortgage (EEM)

Up to **$6,000** can typically be added to a VA loan for qualifying energy improvements — insulation, windows, HVAC, water heaters, weather stripping, solar — documented with contractor bids and expected savings. Amounts above $6,000 require additional VA justification. For a fourplex where you pay heat, this can pay for itself quickly.

:::money Combine the tools
Buy a tired but sound triplex with a VA renovation loan at a discount to a fixed-up building. Use the EEM to cut your owner-paid utility line. Raise rents on turnover. Two years later, the property's NOI supports a refinance — which is your one-time-restoration path to full entitlement in Module 10.
:::

## Taxes, briefly, and get a professional

You are now operating a business. Talk to a CPA who handles rental real estate, ideally one familiar with military clients. Topics worth asking about:

- Depreciation (typically 27.5 years for residential) and how it shelters rental income
- Allocating expenses between your personal unit and the rental units
- Mortgage interest and property tax allocation on a house hack
- What "passive activity loss" limits mean for you and the $25,000 active-participation allowance
- Cost segregation on larger properties
- The eventual capital gains picture, the primary-residence exclusion, and how converting a residence to a rental affects it
- State-level rules where you own versus where you are domiciled

Do not take tax strategy from the internet. This program does not give tax advice.
