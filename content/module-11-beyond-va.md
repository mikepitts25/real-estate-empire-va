## Objectives

- Know the financing products that take over when entitlement is exhausted
- Understand how lenders evaluate you as an investor rather than a homeowner
- Build the lending relationships that make property five through ten possible
- Structure entities and partnerships without breaking your existing loans

## Entitlement is a ladder, not a destination

The VA benefit gets you from zero to three or four properties with almost no capital. That is its job, and it is extraordinary. But at some point the entitlement is committed, and you continue with ordinary investor financing — which you can now access precisely *because* of what the VA loans built: equity, rental income history, reserves, and a track record.

## The product menu

| Product | Down | Qualifies on | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Conventional owner-occupied (Fannie/Freddie)** | 3–5% SFH, 5%+ multi | Your income, DTI | Another house hack after VA entitlement is used |
| **FHA owner-occupied** | 3.5% | Your income, more lenient credit | A house hack when credit is weaker; note lifetime MIP |
| **Conventional investment property** | 20–25% | Your income + 75% of rents | Standard rentals when you have capital |
| **DSCR loan** | 20–25% | **The property's income only** | Scaling past the point where DTI stops you |
| **Portfolio / local bank loan** | 15–25% | Relationship + property | Unusual properties, faster closes, flexible terms |
| **Commercial (5+ units)** | 25–30% | Property NOI, 5-year terms | The step up from small multifamily |
| **Seller financing** | Negotiable | Whatever you negotiate | Tired landlords with no mortgage |
| **HELOC / home equity** | n/a | Your equity + income | Down payments and renovation capital |
| **Hard money / bridge** | 10–25% | The asset | Short-term rehab; expensive, use with an exit plan |

:::money DSCR is the unlock
A DSCR lender asks one primary question: does the property's rent cover the proposed payment, typically at 1.0–1.25×? They generally do not look at your personal DTI, tax returns, or employment. That means **the portfolio's own performance becomes your qualification** — you stop being capped by your W-2. Expect higher rates than conventional, a prepayment penalty, and 20–25% down. Most investors who get past four or five properties get there on DSCR or portfolio debt.
:::

## Fannie Mae's financed-property limits

Conventional lending allows a borrower to have a limited number of financed properties (commonly up to 10 under Fannie Mae guidelines, with tighter reserve and credit requirements past four). Individual lenders often impose lower caps. When you hit a wall, the answer is usually a different lender — a portfolio lender, a credit union, or a DSCR shop — rather than the end of the road.

## Where the down payments come from

Once you are past zero-down, you need capital. Realistic sources, in rough order of preference:

1. **Cash flow from the existing portfolio**, saved deliberately in a dedicated acquisition account
2. **Cash-out refinance or HELOC** on a property with equity — carefully, because it raises that property's payment
3. **Sale of an underperforming property**, ideally via a 1031 exchange to defer capital gains (talk to a CPA and a qualified intermediary — the rules are strict and the timelines short)
4. **Partnerships** — you bring deal-finding, management, and track record; a partner brings capital
5. **Your own savings and disciplined expense control** — unglamorous and reliable
6. **Retirement account strategies** (self-directed IRA real estate) — complex, rule-laden, and easy to get wrong; professional advice required

:::warning Be skeptical of "creative financing" gurus
The market is full of paid courses promising no-money-down infinite-return structures. Some techniques (seller financing, subject-to, lease options) are legitimate and used by real investors. Many are oversold, some run afoul of due-on-sale clauses, and a few are outright fraud. A rule of thumb: if a technique depends on a lender not finding out, do not do it. **Never use a technique that involves misrepresenting occupancy on a VA loan** — you would be trading your earned benefit for a deal.
:::

## Entities, liability, and the due-on-sale question

Common question: should I move properties into an LLC?

Considerations, none of which is legal advice:

- **Residential mortgages, including VA loans, are made to individuals.** Transferring title to an LLC can trigger the due-on-sale clause, and while lenders often do not call the loan, they legally may.
- **Insurance is a real alternative.** A good landlord policy plus a $1–2M umbrella policy provides substantial protection at a fraction of the complexity.
- **Commercial and DSCR lenders frequently prefer or require an LLC**, so entity structure gets easier as you scale.
- **Entity maintenance matters.** An LLC with commingled funds and no records provides little protection.
- **State costs vary widely** — some states charge significant annual franchise fees.

Talk to a real estate attorney in your state before moving any titled property.

## Building lender relationships

At scale, financing is a relationship business. Practical steps:

- Open accounts at **two or three local banks and credit unions** that hold loans in portfolio
- Bring a **one-page portfolio summary** to every conversation: properties, values, loans, NOI, occupancy
- Ask directly: *"What is your appetite for a small multifamily investor with X properties? What are your terms and your limits?"*
- Maintain a **current personal financial statement** and a **rent roll** you can send within an hour
- Keep your books clean enough to hand a lender Schedule E without apologizing

## Knowing when to stop buying

Growth for its own sake wrecks portfolios. Stop or slow when:

- Reserves fall below six months of PITI across the portfolio
- You cannot state each property's occupancy and NOI from memory or a one-page report
- Management quality is slipping and turnover is climbing
- You are relying on appreciation to make deals pencil
- Debt is short-term or adjustable and a rate reset would break cash flow
- Your actual life — family, health, service obligations — is being degraded by the portfolio

**Consolidating, paying down debt, and improving what you own is a legitimate strategy.** Twelve well-run doors that cash flow beats thirty stressed doors, and it is a much better life.
