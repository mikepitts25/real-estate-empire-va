## Objectives

- Understand how VA underwriting differs from conventional — especially residual income
- Calculate your own DTI and residual income
- Know what to fix, and in what order, before you apply
- Screen lenders with questions that separate the competent from the order-takers

## The VA has no minimum credit score

Officially, the VA does not set a minimum FICO score. **Lenders do.** Most VA lenders overlay a minimum around 620; some go to 580, a handful lower. Better scores buy better pricing.

What matters more than the number, for VA underwriting:

- **Twelve months of clean recent history.** Recent lates hurt far more than old ones.
- **Bankruptcy:** generally a two-year seasoning after Chapter 7 discharge; Chapter 13 can qualify after 12 months of on-time plan payments with trustee approval.
- **Foreclosure:** generally two years. Note that a prior VA foreclosure also consumed entitlement that may not be restorable until the VA's loss is repaid.
- **Collections and judgments:** the VA is more flexible than conventional, particularly for medical collections, but open judgments usually must be resolved or on a documented payment plan.

:::tip Fastest legitimate score moves
Pay revolving balances below 30% (ideally below 10%) of limits and let one statement cycle report; dispute genuine reporting errors; do not close old accounts; do not open new credit during the 120 days before applying. A 40-point move in 60 days from utilization alone is common.
:::

## Debt-to-income: the 41% guideline

VA guidance uses **41% DTI** as a benchmark, not a hard ceiling. Loans above 41% are routinely approved when residual income exceeds the requirement by at least 20% and compensating factors exist (long job stability, significant reserves, minimal payment shock, excellent credit).

```
DTI = (proposed PITI + all monthly debt payments) ÷ gross monthly qualifying income
```

Practically: automotive debt is the number one thing standing between veterans and a fourplex. A $750 truck payment consumes roughly $1,800 of monthly qualifying income at 41% DTI.

## Residual income: the VA's secret weapon

This is the rule that makes VA loans both safer and, for the right borrower, more generous than conventional. After the mortgage payment, all debts, taxes, and estimated utilities, the VA requires that a specific dollar amount remain each month — **residual income** — scaled by family size, region, and loan size.

Illustrative figures for loan amounts of $80,000 and above (**confirm the current tables in the VA Lenders Handbook, M26-7, Chapter 4 — these change**):

| Family size | Northeast | Midwest | South | West |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ~$450 | ~$441 | ~$441 | ~$491 |
| 2 | ~$755 | ~$738 | ~$738 | ~$823 |
| 3 | ~$909 | ~$889 | ~$889 | ~$990 |
| 4 | ~$1,025 | ~$1,003 | ~$1,003 | ~$1,158 |
| 5 | ~$1,062 | ~$1,039 | ~$1,039 | ~$1,194 |

Utilities are usually estimated at roughly **$0.14 per square foot per month** of the property. Note the trap for multi-unit buyers: a fourplex is a large building, so the utility estimate is large, which raises your residual income requirement.

## Income that counts

VA underwriting is friendlier than most to military income:

- **BAH and BAS** count as income, and because they are non-taxable, lenders commonly **gross them up** (often by 25%) for qualification.
- **VA disability compensation** counts, is non-taxable, and may be grossed up. It is also stable income by definition.
- **Drill pay** for Guard/Reserve typically requires a history and likelihood of continuance.
- **Self-employment** generally requires two years of returns.
- **Future rental income** from the subject property is the big one — see below.

### Using rental income from the units you are buying

To count rent from the other units of the multi-unit property you are purchasing, VA guidance generally requires **both**:

1. **Cash reserves** sufficient to cover approximately **six months of PITI**, and
2. **Documented prior experience as a landlord** — managing rental units or a comparable background in property management and maintenance

When allowed, lenders typically credit **75% of verified market rent** (an appraiser's Form 1007/1025 rent schedule, or existing leases), the remaining 25% being the vacancy and maintenance haircut.

:::warning Plan for the chicken-and-egg problem
Many first-time buyers cannot use the subject property's rental income because they have no landlord history. That means you must qualify for the whole payment on your own income. Two responses: (a) buy a duplex rather than a fourplex first so the payment is qualifiable, or (b) build documented landlord experience first — even managing a single rented room or a family member's property with real leases and a management agreement can help. Ask your lender exactly what documentation they will accept **before** you go under contract.
:::

## Reserves

The VA does not require reserves on a single-family owner-occupied purchase, but for **3–4 unit properties lenders commonly require six months of PITI in reserves** — and require them again if you want the rental income credit. Reserves may include vested retirement funds at a discount, but generally not the proceeds of a cash-out from the subject property.

Start building this now. It is the one place in this strategy where you genuinely need cash.

## Choosing a lender

Most loan officers have done twenty VA single-family purchases and zero VA fourplexes. That difference will cost you a deal. Interview at least three, and ask:

- "How many VA loans on 2–4 unit properties have you closed in the past 12 months?"
- "What is your credit score overlay for a fourplex?"
- "Under what conditions will you let me count the subject property's rental income, and what documentation do you require for landlord experience?"
- "What reserves will you require on a 3–4 unit?"
- "Do you underwrite in-house, and what is your average close time?"
- "What is your lender credit structure, and can you price a slightly higher rate to cover closing costs?"
- "Will you give me a fully underwritten pre-approval, not just a pre-qualification?"

:::tip Fully underwritten pre-approval wins offers
A pre-approval where a human underwriter has already reviewed your income, assets, and credit — with only the property outstanding — makes your offer competitive with cash-ish terms. Sellers' agents in many markets still carry stale myths about VA loans being slow or fussy. A strong underwritten letter plus a lender willing to call the listing agent personally neutralizes that.
:::

## Compare offers with a Loan Estimate

Ask every lender for a **Loan Estimate** on the same scenario, the same day. Compare page 2 line by line: origination charges, discount points, and the lender credit. Rate alone is not the comparison.

## Action items

- [ ] Pull all three credit reports and dispute any genuine errors
- [ ] Pay revolving balances under 30% of limits and let one cycle report
- [ ] List every monthly debt payment and calculate your DTI
- [ ] Run the residual income calculator for a realistic target property
- [ ] Open a dedicated reserve account and set an automatic transfer toward six months of PITI
- [ ] Interview three VA lenders using the questions above; keep notes
- [ ] Obtain a fully underwritten pre-approval and a Loan Estimate from your top two
