## Objectives

By the end of this module you will be able to:

- Explain what the VA "guaranty" is and why it produces zero-down financing
- List the features of the benefit that matter for portfolio building
- Identify the funding fee that applies to you — including whether you are exempt
- Request your Certificate of Eligibility (COE)

## What the VA actually does

The Department of Veterans Affairs does not lend you money. With one exception (the Native American Direct Loan), the VA **guarantees a portion of a loan made by a private lender**. That guaranty — traditionally 25% of the loan amount — is what replaces your down payment.

A conventional lender wants 20% down because that equity is their cushion if you default and they have to foreclose. The VA guaranty provides an equivalent cushion. The lender's risk position on a $0-down VA loan resembles their position on a 25%-down conventional loan. That is the entire mechanism, and understanding it explains every rule that follows: **the VA's rules exist to protect the guaranty.**

## The features that build empires

| Feature | What it means | Why it matters to you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **No down payment** | 100% financing up to the reasonable value of the property | Capital is the binding constraint for most investors. It isn't for you. |
| **No mortgage insurance** | No monthly PMI or MIP, ever | Saves roughly $150–$400/month on a typical loan versus FHA or low-down conventional — that is cash flow |
| **1–4 units allowed** | You may buy a duplex, triplex, or fourplex, occupying one unit | This is the single most important line in this program |
| **Competitive rates** | VA rates typically price at or below conventional | Lower payment, better cash flow, better DTI |
| **Reusable** | Entitlement can be restored or partially reused | The benefit is a cycle, not a one-shot |
| **Assumable** | A qualified buyer — including a non-veteran — may assume your loan | A 3% assumable loan is a powerful asset when market rates are high |
| **No prepayment penalty** | Pay it down or off whenever | Freedom to refinance or sell |
| **Foreclosure avoidance help** | VA loan technicians can intervene with servicers | A genuine safety net |

:::money The cash-flow math of no PMI
On a $400,000 loan, FHA mortgage insurance can run roughly $250–$290 per month for the life of the loan. A VA loan charges a one-time funding fee instead, which you can finance. Over a 10-year hold that is roughly $30,000+ of difference — per property.
:::

## What the benefit will not do

Be equally clear on the limits. The VA loan:

- **Is for primary residences only.** You cannot use it to buy a pure rental, a vacation home, or a property you never intend to live in. Module 8 covers occupancy in detail — this is the rule people break and it is the rule with criminal consequences.
- **Will not finance more than four units** on a single VA purchase (exception: two or more eligible veterans buying jointly may combine entitlement for additional units).
- **Will not finance a property that fails Minimum Property Requirements (MPRs).** Gut-rehab properties usually cannot be bought with a standard VA purchase loan.
- **Does not cap your interest rate or guarantee approval.** Lenders still underwrite you.
- **Does not eliminate closing costs** — though Module 7 shows how to get someone else to pay them.

## The funding fee

Most borrowers pay a one-time **VA funding fee**, which funds the program so taxpayers do not. It is a percentage of the loan amount, and it can be financed into the loan (which is how a true $0-out-of-pocket purchase works).

The schedule below reflects the rates in effect since April 7, 2023. **Confirm current rates at va.gov before relying on them.**

| Loan type | Down payment | First use | Subsequent use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase / construction | Less than 5% | 2.15% | 3.30% |
| Purchase / construction | 5% – 9.99% | 1.50% | 1.50% |
| Purchase / construction | 10% or more | 1.25% | 1.25% |
| Cash-out refinance | Any | 2.15% | 3.30% |
| IRRRL (streamline refi) | n/a | 0.50% | 0.50% |
| Loan assumption | n/a | 0.50% | 0.50% |

:::tip You may be exempt — check
The funding fee is generally waived for veterans receiving VA compensation for a service-connected disability, those rated eligible to receive compensation from a pre-discharge exam or review, Purple Heart recipients serving on active duty, and certain surviving spouses. A 10% rating waives the fee just as a 100% rating does. On a $450,000 subsequent-use purchase, that exemption is worth roughly **$14,850 on a single deal** — and it applies every time you use the benefit.

If you are close to a rating decision, this alone can be worth timing your purchase around. And if you paid a funding fee and were later awarded a retroactive rating covering the closing date, you may be entitled to a refund — ask your lender and the VA Regional Loan Center.
:::

## Eligibility, briefly

Eligibility is based on service. General service-length thresholds (with many exceptions for era, discharge type, and service-connected disability discharge):

- **Active duty:** typically 90 continuous days during wartime, or 181 days during peacetime; 24 continuous months or the full period called to active duty for post-1980 enlistees
- **National Guard and Reserves:** typically 90 days of active-duty service, or six creditable years in the Selected Reserve or National Guard plus a qualifying discharge
- **Surviving spouses:** of a service member who died in the line of duty or from a service-connected disability, and certain others

Discharge generally must be other than dishonorable. **The VA is the only authority on your eligibility.** Do not take a lender's, a forum's, or this page's word for it — pull your COE.

## Get your Certificate of Eligibility

The COE is the document proving to a lender that you are eligible and showing how much entitlement you have available. Three routes:

1. **Online, fastest:** log in at [va.gov](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/how-to-request-coe/) and request it. Many veterans receive it immediately.
2. **Through a lender:** most VA-approved lenders can pull it through the WebLGY system in minutes. This is often the fastest path of all.
3. **By mail:** VA Form 26-1880, with supporting service documents (DD-214, statement of service, NGB-22).

Read it carefully when it arrives. Note any prior entitlement charged, any funding fee exemption status, and any conditions. Module 2 turns those numbers into buying power.

:::warning The most common COE surprise
Veterans who used the benefit years ago — and sold the home — often assume their entitlement is gone or automatically restored. Neither is reliably true. A prior loan that was assumed by someone else, or paid off without the paperwork being filed, may still show entitlement charged. Find out now, not while under contract.
:::
